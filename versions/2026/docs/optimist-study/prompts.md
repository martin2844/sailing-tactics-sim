## A

Use case: stylized-concept. Create a technically faithful low-polygon 3D Optimist racing dinghy reference for a serious sailing simulator. The supplied real North Sails and Far East photographs are geometry references, not an edit target. Preserve the actual boat and rig silhouette. Hull length 2.36m, beam 1.12m: short broad box/pram, flat bow transom and flat stern, thin gunwales, angular hard chines, white fiberglass, large open interior with three gray buoyancy bags, daggerboard trunk, thwart and thin hiking straps. No pointed bow, no thick toy bevels, no cabin. Sail MUST match the photographs: four corners, short vertical luff on the mast, steeply rising short head to the peak, then a long sloping leech down to the clew. NOT a rectangular square sail and NOT a triangular Bermuda sail. Proportion guide in sail-plane metres relative to tack: tack (0,0), throat (0,1.73), peak (0.87,2.60), clew (2.04,0.16); thus the peak is much higher than the masthead, the head rises about 45 degrees, and the foot is slightly rising. Hull length 2.36m compared to sail foot ~2.04m. Slender unstayed mast about 2.26m from its floor step, diagonal thin sprit from low on mast to high peak, horizontal boom tied along foot, realistic thin mainsheet, only two SHORT leech battens (not full-width horizontal black stripes), understated sail stitching and a small clear window near the lower luff. Matte white Dacron sail with modest aerodynamic camber and subtle geometry facets. Dark gray spars. Desaturated restrained materials, neutral daylight, precise industrial/game-art rendering. Human if present has natural anatomy, normal small head and hands, serious racing posture, navy wetsuit and dark life jacket, no cartoon/chibi, no orange mascot, no helmet. Ignore orange analytical diagonal overlaid on reference photos: that is NOT a real rope. No fake dimension labels or marketing text, no distorted wide angle, no exaggerated rounding. Frame whole boat and rig with margins. Variant A: dry technical studio reference, one boat without a sailor so its structure is fully readable. A large exact side elevation viewed from starboard plus a smaller three-quarter stern view on a pale gray studio backdrop, same boat and scale proportions. Refined low-poly mesh facets and minimal material noise, crisp hard hull chines, lightweight rim. Authentic Optimist identity comes from silhouette, not logos. Entire rig in both views.
