# Review of the square-root certificate

The square-root helper accepts finite positive binary64 values in `[2^-100,2^100]`, retaining signed zero separately. Its candidate is accepted only when the rounded square lies between half and twice the input. This makes the first square residual exact by Sterbenz's lemma and bounds all Dekker split/product intermediates away from overflow and underflow. Dekker's squared-product correction is exact in this domain. Subtracting that correction gives a single RN binary64 rounding of the true squared residual. The added `4u` residual margin covers its exact value despite the subsequent bound arithmetic. A zero residual cannot hide underflow in this bounded domain, so it identifies an exact positive root.

For candidate `c` and neighbor spacings `l` and `h`, the exact squared midpoint displacements are `-c*l + l*l/4` and `c*h + h*h/4`. The helper obtains the binade from the binary64 image and halves the lower spacing at powers of two. Products by these dyadic spacings are exact. The omitted quadratic is at most `2^-54` of the corresponding product. Moving each limit inward by `2^-50` of that product, even after rounding the limit calculation, dominates the quadratic and rounding errors. Strict residual inclusion therefore proves that the candidate lies inside the correct RN53 cell. Near-halfway cases decline to the existing exact root implementation.
