# Bounded double-double candidate review

This review applies to the source hashes in `double-double-review.json`. It is an absolute-error argument for the two bounded candidates, not a claim that the helper module implements a general 106-bit floating-point format. The executable diagnostic separately compares the unrounded fixed112 integers with the previous 224-bit algorithms.

Write `u = 2^-53`. All coefficients, anchors and fixed-point inputs are converted by rounding a high binary64 component, then rounding the exact integer residual. Their represented sum therefore differs from the fixed-point value by at most `u^2` times its magnitude. The multiplication by the conversion's power of two is exact. Converting a pair back to fixed112 truncates each component, adding less than two fixed112 units of absolute error.

For the operands used here, the high components are at most 1.001 in magnitude, except the atan denominator (at most 2.001) and the final pi subtraction (at most 3.142). The low components remain at most `u` times the high component plus the preceding absolute rounding error. Thus they are bounded by `2u` for the unit-sized operations. The relevant elementary absolute bounds are deliberately loose:

- Addition or subtraction: `64u^2` for the unit-sized operands, and `128u^2` for the final pi operations.
- Multiplication: `128u^2` for the unit-sized operands.
- The atan division: `256u^2` when the numerator is at most .0081 and the denominator is between 1 and 2.001.

The first high-component sum and its TwoSum residual represent the exact sum. Rounding the additions of the two low components introduces an error of order `u^2`, because the three residual terms are each of order `u`. Renormalization is exact when the high sum dominates its correction. In the cancellation case its possible error is bounded absolutely by `4u` times the correction, which is again of order `u^2` relative to the original operands. This handles the cancellation in the division remainder without assuming a relative-error bound for that remainder.

The split high-component multiplication is the error-free Dekker product in this bounded normal domain. The remaining cross products are of order `u`; their roundings and accumulation have error of order `u^2`. Bounding every cross product by its magnitude and allowing all accumulation roundings is below the stated 128-unit budget. For division, the first rounded quotient is at most .0081. Subtracting its product from the numerator leaves a remainder of order `u`; rounding the remainder sum and correction division therefore adds order `u^2`. Using the high denominator instead of the complete pair in the correction introduces order `u^2` too. The 256-unit budget includes the multiplication and subtraction errors in this remainder.

The half-pi table initialization is an exact power-of-two scaling of the represented pi pair. Its multiplication by .5 therefore does not introduce a non-unit multiplication error into the division or polynomial budgets.

These transforms do not approach binary64 overflow. Splitting multiplies a high component by `2^27 + 1`. Fixed224 residual inputs are dyadic multiples of `2^-224`; atan first quantizes its ratio to fixed112. Even the smallest nonzero sine residual and its square are at least `2^-224` and `2^-448`. Multiplying these by the represented coefficients and their low components stays above binary64 underflow; zero components are harmless. Consequently the error-free-transform argument does not silently assume an unavailable normal product.

For sine and cosine, the nearest dyadic anchor leaves a residual below .0081. Its squared residual is below .000066. Horner evaluation therefore contracts earlier coefficient and arithmetic errors at each step. Including input conversion and squaring, each polynomial error is below `512u^2`. The final residual product and the two anchor identities have error below `2048u^2`. The degree-11 sine remainder is below `.0081^13 / 13! < 2^-122`; the degree-10 cosine remainder is below `.0081^12 / 12! < 2^-112`.

For atan, ratio quantization contributes less than `2^-112`. Numerator and denominator formation, followed by the corrected division, bound the residual error below `400u^2`. Squaring contributes below `135u^2`; Horner's same contraction bounds its polynomial error below `512u^2`. The residual product, anchor addition and both possible pi subtractions keep the final absolute error below `2048u^2`. The degree-15 remainder is below `.0081^17 / 17 < 2^-118`.

The additional binary64-operand ratio shortcut uses exact positive `A <= B`, with both operands between `2^-100` and `2^100`. Its first quotient `q = RN(A/B)` is normal, between `2^-200` and 1. Multiplication of the two single-component pairs `B` and `q` is an error-free TwoProd: their product is close to `A`, and even the split cross products remain normal. Neither splitting (at most `2^127`) nor any correction approaches overflow. The subtraction from `A` is a cancellation between neighboring numbers, so the high-component difference is exact. Its renormalized remainder differs from the exact `A - Bq` by less than `3u^2 A`. Summing its components and dividing by `B` adds less than another `3u^2 A/B`. Adding this correction to `q` is an exact TwoSum. Thus the corrected ratio error is conservatively bounded by `8u^2 A/B`, and by `8u^2` absolutely. This replaces the previous conversion-plus-fixed112 ratio error; the later residual remains below its existing `400u^2` budget and the final `2^-94` bound is unchanged.

This shortcut runs only when both immutable operands expose an exact finite binary64 carrier within that range. Zero, non-binary64 m80 values and operands outside the range keep the previous path. A ratio so small that its fixed112 enclosure cannot certify m80 also keeps the previous path; the preliminary attempt does not alter the control word or operand values.

The old truncated224 series and Machin pi contribute less than 10,000 original224 units, insignificant at this scale. Adding the fixed112 truncations and both analytic remainders leaves either candidate within `4096u^2 = 2^-94` of the old fixed224 integer. The production radius is `2^-88`, a factor of 64 larger. This margin does not depend on the x87 arithmetic precision control: the candidates use binary64 operations, and certification always compares one m80 rounding of the exact signed fixed-point endpoints.

The fixed112 certificate rounds the smaller nonzero magnitude once. Every bounded endpoint is a normal m80 value between `2^-112` and 4, so no overflow or subnormal rounding is omitted. Comparing the larger magnitude with the exact upper midpoint of that rounding cell, accepting equality only for an even significand, is equivalent to comparing both public constructor results. Negative intervals use their ordered magnitudes. An interval crossing zero is declined.

For `Float80.toNumber()`, the prior 53-bit rounding has already produced an integer significand of at most 53 bits and a unit exponent of at least -1074. Converting this integer and its power of two is exact. Their product is the already-rounded binary64 image, or infinity when the rounded image overflows. A zero significand is handled separately to retain its sign.

The diagnostic tests 20,000 raw sine/cosine components, 10,000 fixed-ratio atan candidates and 10,000 binary64-pair atan candidates, balanced across all four quadrants. The source-pinned JSON records their maximum fixed112 errors, versus the production radius of 16,777,216. The binary64 corpus includes both magnitude-domain edges and ratios down to `2^-200`; explicit guard instrumentation verifies that signed zero and out-of-domain inputs decline. These observations support the bounded argument; they are not a substitute for its domain assumptions or for the independent native frame fixtures.

The additional Number sine/cosine shortcut retains the original 66-bit x87 pi, rather than binary64 pi. The exact half-pi dyadic is `0x3243f6a8885a308d3 * 2^-65`; splitting it into a rounded 53-bit high component and its exact residual leaves at most 13 significant bits in the low component. For finite binary64 inputs with magnitude between .5 and `2^20`, the candidate quadrant has at most 20 bits. The high-component product is an error-free TwoProd, and the low-component product has at most 33 bits and is exact. Their error terms have a span shorter than 53 bits, so their accumulation is exact too. Subtracting the original binary64 input and comparing with the quarter-pi boundary use dyadics supported within exponents -66 through 21. The two components retain every bit of this reduction. Correcting a quotient on either side of the quarter boundary preserves the original signed integer-division convention. The same certified polynomial receives this exact reduced angle; its prior error budget is unchanged. Small residuals and failed certificates retain the original path. `number-reduction-review.json` independently compares 100,000 cases, including adjacent binary64 values around quarter boundaries, against the original fixed224 reduction and series.

The final direct numeric atan entry uses the same bounded binary64 ratio and candidate as the immutable-operand entry. The diagnostic now also compares its 30,000 m80 outputs with the original224 implementation, across all three precision-control words. Signed-zero axes and finite values outside its domain load the original binary64 images from left to right and retain the original calculation.

The separate PC53 mixed multiplication certificate splits the exact 64-bit significand into a 53-bit high component `H` and an eleven-bit tail `T`, with `0 <= T/H < 2u`. Its bounded operands keep the high product, split cross products, nonzero tail product and error terms normal and finite. Dekker TwoProd represents `HB` exactly as `p + e`. Rounding `TB` loses less than `2u^2 HB`; rounding its addition to `e` loses less than another `4u^2 HB`. The candidate is the rounded sum of `p` and this correction. Sterbenz subtraction makes `p - candidate` exact, and rounding the final correction distance contributes less than another `4u^2 HB`. A conservative total budget is `256u^2 p = p * 2^-98`, substantially above these losses.

The implementation tests the rounded distance against both binary64 rounding-cell midpoints with twice that budget, covering the boundary-subtraction roundings too. It derives spacing from the exact binary64 exponent and fraction bits. Immediately at a power of two, the lower half-cell is half the upper half-cell. Both comparisons are strict, so exact ties and uncertain cells keep the original BigInt product. Applying the operand signs after the positive certificate preserves the symmetric negative cell. Other precision modes, zeros and operands outside the bounded domain retain the original multiplication. `mixed-multiply-review.json` compares 60,000 results from 20,000 vectors, including every eleven-bit tail at power-of-two neighbors, plus alternate precision modes and unchanged native multiply images. Its leaf timing measures this method alone.
